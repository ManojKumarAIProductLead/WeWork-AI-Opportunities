Three pillars — problem → user → solution → KPI
1
Member Experience & Monetization
For members — individuals to startups · Owned by Product & Community teams
Problem
Servicing is fragmented across app, front desk, and email. Value-added services go undiscovered. Community is events-only, not a network.
Solution bets
WorkPal — one-stop agentic hub for every member need + contextual cross-sell. CommunityGraph — AI member matching as a retention moat.
Why AI wins here
Intent understanding + member context = each interaction becomes a service and a sales channel at zero marginal cost.
KPIs moved: retention/churn · average ticket size (LTV) · VAS attach rate
2
Operational Excellence
For internal teams — Sales/BD, Community Ops, Finance · Owned by Revenue & Ops teams
Problem
Pipeline hygiene, quotes, contracts, renewals, seat changes, and billing are manual and CRM-bound. Ops teams react to tickets instead of anticipating them.
Solution bets
DealDesk — AI agents wrapping the existing CRM/CPQ/billing stack with human approval gates. SmartPass + SenseSpace — physical signals automating ops workflows.
Why AI wins here
We don't replace Salesforce — we wrap it. Agents do the drafting, chasing, and reconciling; humans approve. Fast to ship, low integration risk.
KPIs moved: sales cycle time · cost-to-serve per seat · renewal lead time · long-term leasing mix
3
Space & Asset Intelligence
For enterprise clients + Supply/Real-Estate & Workspace Design teams
Problem
Enterprises can't see how their space is used. WeWork's own expansion decisions lean on relationships and gut feel. Physical signals (occupancy, energy, environment) are uncaptured.
Solution bets
SpaceIQ — utilization analytics for enterprise clients. LocationIQ — ML/GIS site-selection scoring, refreshed monthly. SenseSpace — sensor layer feeding both.
Why AI wins here
The data (badge swipes, bookings, sensors, market signals) already exists or is cheap to capture. Models turn it into pricing power and capex discipline.
KPIs moved: revenue per sq ft · enterprise retention · new-center ramp time · energy cost per sq ft
The hard question — adoption comes before everything
Why the app sits at 40% — and the fix
None of these bets pay off without a daily-active member base — and today there isn't one. The features exist; adoption is still 40% with low engagement. That's not a feature gap, it's proof the app isn't on the critical path of anyone's day. The fix isn't more features — it's making the app the key to the one action no one can skip: getting through the door.

Global reference point
WeWork Global already validated the direction: WeWork Workplace (built with Yardi, ~600 companies and 75,000+ licenses by early 2024) proved enterprises will pay for space-management software, and the March 2024 global app update made the WeWork App a one-stop hub for booking, access, and workspace insights. WeWork India can leapfrog both — neither is AI-native. The pitch isn't "copy global" — it's "own the AI layer global hasn't built, tuned for India's GCC-heavy demand."

Why this is feasible, not sky-throwing
Build on what exists
Every bet wraps current systems — the app, access control, CRM, badge data. No rip-and-replace. Agents draft; humans approve.
90-day pilot rhythm
Each prototype maps to a single-center or single-account pilot shippable in one quarter, with the 2 success measures defined on its demo page.
Sequenced by ROI confidence
Start where data exists today (WorkPal, SpaceIQ, DealDesk) → instrument the physical layer (Phygital) → compound into LocationIQ and CommunityGraph.
