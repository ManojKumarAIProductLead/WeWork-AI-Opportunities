Why the app sits at 40% — and the one thing that fixes it

The reframe
The 40% is the most useful data we have. It proves adoption isn't downstream of features — we shipped those. Adoption is downstream of necessity. You can't feature your way out of an adoption problem.
Why features don't move the needle here
The app is a vitamin, not a painkiller
Every job the app does has a frictionless physical alternative that already works: the keycard opens the door, UPI pays the café, the badge runs the printer, wifi is saved. People reach for what's already in their hand.
Nothing sits on the critical path
No single unavoidable daily action requires the app. So members download it once to do one thing — book a desk — and never route back. That's exactly the dormancy pattern we see.
The employee never chose WeWork
In a B2B2C model the company picks WeWork; the employee is just assigned a desk. There's no onboarding, no moment of magic, no reason to form a habit — the activation moment that every consumer app engineers simply doesn't exist.
We're measuring the wrong thing
Downloads are a vanity ceiling and "we have features" is a vanity input. The real metric: does a member take one action through the app every day that they can't easily do any other way? Today, no.
The fix — put the app on the one path no one can skip
1
Make the phone the key
Entry is the only 100%-frequency daily action — make the app own it
Don't make the app better — make it necessary. When the turnstile opens best (or only) via the phone, download stops being a marketing campaign and becomes a precondition of walking in. This is a forcing function, not a feature: it converts the single unavoidable action of the day into a daily app open — the habit everything else (café, VAS, networking, stored value) has to be built on top of.
Precedent: Disney didn't drive app adoption by adding features — the MagicBand/app became the key to your room, rides, and payments, so there was no parallel path and adoption was near-total. Airlines grew mobile boarding passes by making the phone the credential and optimizing the gate around it until paper felt worse. Adoption follows the credential, not the feature.
It's a managed migration, not a Monday mandate:
Phase 1
Phone access is genuinely faster & becomes the default; card still works
Phase 2
New joiners & new centers launch phone-first; card is fallback only
Phase 3
Card deprecated over quarters; designed-for edge cases (guests, devices)
2
Manufacture a day-one activation moment
Use unavoidable first-day friction as the trigger, reward it instantly
The employee has no "aha" because they never chose WeWork. So engineer one at first contact: day one, the only way to get your desk, your wifi, and through the door is a 30-second app setup — and that setup immediately does something delightful (access pass lands in your wallet, your desk shows on a map, first coffee's on the house, calendar synced so the app already knows your week). This fixes both the 60% who never download and the dormancy in the 40% who did.
The B2B2C handoff: the admin gets the whole company onto the app for administrative reasons (provisioning, billing, seat management) — that scales downloads one-admin-to-many-employees. Then the daily-utility + context engine keeps the individual engaged for their own reasons. Acquisition is B2B-led; engagement is B2C-led; both run through one app.
3
Re-anchor the metric on the daily power-action
Stop counting downloads; count daily-active against one action
Pick the single "power action" — entry — make the app unambiguously own it, and measure daily-active against that, not against a feature count. Once one daily action genuinely requires the app, downloads and DAU stop being targets you chase and become outputs that take care of themselves. Then — and only then — the café order-ahead, stored-value wallet, and context-driven nudges have a daily-open habit to attach to.
Success measures & business case
Measure 1 — necessity
Daily app-open rate among present members
Target: of members who badge in on a given day, the share who opened the app that day climbs toward 100% as phone-access rolls out — the true adoption signal.
Measure 2 — activation
Day-1 setup completion for new members
Target: new joiners completing app setup within their first visit (the manufactured activation moment) — fixes the 60% who never download at the source.
Business case: adoption isn't the goal — it's the gate. None of the monetization bets (VAS marketplace, cross-sell, CommunityGraph, stored-value wallet) can pay off without a daily-active base, and today there isn't one. Making the app the key unlocks the install and daily-open habit that every other revenue lever in this portfolio depends on. It's the lowest-glamour, highest-leverage move in the roadmap.
"We've proven features don't drive adoption here — we shipped them and we're at 40%. Adoption isn't downstream of features, it's downstream of necessity. So we stop adding capabilities people can route around, and put the app on the one path no one can skip: getting through the door."
