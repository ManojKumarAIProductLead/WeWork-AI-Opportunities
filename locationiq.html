LocationIQ
ML + GIS scoring of candidate micro-markets for center expansion — combining mobility data, enterprise cluster density, talent supply, and demand signals into a single defensible score per site.
Honest framing: a property investment is a low-frequency, high-stakes decision (₹15–25 Cr capex + a multi-year lease liability per center). LocationIQ is therefore not a daily AI dashboard — it's a long-term GIS study powered by ML models refreshed monthly, feeding a quarterly investment-committee pack. The AI's job is to make the shortlist defensible and kill bad sites early, not to replace deal judgment.

How it works — architecture
1 · Data sources
Monthly ingestion
Telecom mobility panels · transit/POI data · job postings · broker rent comps · competitor openings · WeWork demand (searches, waitlists, member commute origins)
2 · Feature layer
GIS feature store
H3 hexagonal grid over each city; 40+ features per cell, versioned monthly for back-testing
3 · Models
Score + forecast
Gradient-boosted site score trained on existing-center ramp history · hedonic rent model · 12-month occupancy forecast with confidence bands
4 · Output
Quarterly IC pack
Ranked shortlist with score breakdown, sensitivity analysis, and "what would change our mind" triggers — human decision stays with the deal team

Success measures & business case
Measure 1 — ramp speed
Months to 70% occupancy for new centers
Target: model-backed sites ramp ≥25% faster than the historical average. Back-test on the last 10 openings before trusting it forward.
Measure 2 — forecast accuracy
12-month occupancy forecast error (MAPE)
Target: <12% error on predicted vs. actual occupancy at month 12. This is the trust metric that earns the model a seat at the IC table.
Business case: one avoided bad site saves ₹15–25 Cr in capex plus a multi-year lease liability; one accelerated ramp on a 1,500-seat center is worth ~₹3–4 Cr in earlier revenue. Even a model that improves the hit rate of 4–6 annual decisions by one site pays for itself many times over. Cost to build: 2 engineers + 1 data scientist for one quarter, mostly on data acquisition.
